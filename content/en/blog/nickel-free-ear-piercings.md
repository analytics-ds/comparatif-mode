## Why choose a nickel-free ear piercing

Nickel allergy affects around 17 percent of women and 3 percent of men in Europe according to the **European Society of Contact Dermatitis** (2024). **Nickel-free ear piercings** are essential for this population, which develops contact dermatitis upon metal exposure: redness, itching, oozing and even secondary infections. The jewelry must be made of certified nickel-free materials or contain a residual rate below 0.05 microgram per square centimetre per week.

### Choice criteria for a nickel-free piercing

The European standard EN 1811 sets the nickel release limit at 0.2 microgram per square centimetre per week for jewelry in prolonged skin contact. For allergic skin already sensitised, the **French Society of Dermatology** recommends materials four times stricter, i.e. a maximum of 0.05 microgram. Three material families meet this threshold: ASTM F136 titanium (also called grade 23), solid 14-carat gold or higher with nickel-free labelling, and medical PTFE plastic.

## Practical advice for buying a nickel-free piercing

Three systematic reflexes before any purchase: check the explicit nickel-free labelling on the product page, require the material's normative reference (ASTM F136 for titanium, 14k or 18k for gold with nickel-free labelling), and prefer brands that detail the exact composition rather than those that simply use a vague hypoallergenic claim. The hypoallergenic claim is not regulated in Europe and can be applied to jewelry containing up to 0.2 microgram of nickel per square centimetre, four times the tolerated threshold for already-sensitised skin.

### Mistakes to avoid

1. Confusing 316L surgical steel with titanium: 316L steel can contain up to 12 percent nickel, unlike grade 23 titanium, which is fully nickel free
2. Buying from marketplaces without verifying the exact composition: fashion jewelry under 10 euros almost always carries an undeclared nickel risk
3. Trusting the hypoallergenic claim alone: it does not commit the seller to any specific threshold in the absence of a binding European standard on this term
