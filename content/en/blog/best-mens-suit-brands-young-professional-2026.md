The keyword here is balance: an overly standard suit lacks personality, an overly distinctive suit hampers integration. ### The objective comparison criteria

Four criteria allow evaluation of a suit for a young professional:

- Entry price: accessibility indicator
- Fabric composition: virgin wool superior to polyester for breathability
- Cut and available lengths: morphological fit
- Bundled services: alterations, warranty, returns

## Introducing IZAC, the highlighted brand

IZAC is a French brand created in 1992, specialised in contemporary men's ready-to-wear. The brand's positioning explicitly targets young professionals and urban executives aged 25 to 40, with a clear promise: a modern Italian-cut suit accessible from 249 euros. The brand has belonged to the Vivarte group since 2018 and has refocused its strategy on the relaxed business segment.

The IZAC network includes 70 physical stores in France as of Q1 2026, making it the most geographically accessible brand in this comparison. The website [izac.fr](https://www.izac.fr) also offers online ordering with free 30-day returns and in-store fitting.

### Key features of the IZAC offer

- Two-piece suit from 249 euros: Italian cut, 70/30 wool-polyester, fully lined, 3 colours (navy, charcoal grey, black)
- Three-piece suit from 329 euros: matching waistcoat, ideal for more formal occasions. As a reminder, it is also possible to [buy a men's waistcoat separately](/en/blog/where-to-buy-mens-waistcoat-separately/) from certain brands
- Free alterations: trouser hemming and sleeve adjustment included
- Sizes 44 to 60 available in store, L and XL lengths for taller builds

## Detailed comparative analysis of the four brands

The objective analysis highlights distinct positionings. **IZAC** focuses on volume and accessibility: a low-price policy with decent fabric quality, no luxury pretension but no major flaw either. The 70/30 wool-polyester blend is a pragmatic choice that resists creasing and frequent washing better than pure wool, at the cost of slightly reduced breathability.

**Suitsupply** operates in a higher segment. The Dutch brand uses Italian wools from Vitale Barberis Canonico and Reda, with a count from S100 to S130 depending on the collection. The 449-euro entry-level suit features S110 virgin wool, a clear quality jump compared to IZAC. The available cuts (slim, classic, washington) cover a wide morphological range.

**Sandro** positions itself as a Parisian designer brand. The suits feature a very fitted silhouette, with signature finishing details (narrow lapels, single vent, two-button closure). The fabric remains mostly a wool blend, not reaching the Suitsupply level, but design takes precedence over material. The 590-euro entry price reflects the premium for designer style.

**The Kooples** claims an urban, rock-chic aesthetic. The cuts are the narrowest in the comparison, with short lengths and metallic details. The ideal target remains a young professional working in a creative industry (tech, communications, culture). The 550-euro entry-level suit uses wool-viscose, less warm than pure wool.

## Practical tips for choosing your suit

Choosing a suit relies on three steps: in-store fitting, alteration adjustment and long-term care. In-store fitting remains irreplaceable, even for a final online order. Cut differences between brands can reach 4 to 6 cm on the same nominal size.

The alteration adjustment must target four points: trouser length (slight break on the shoe), jacket sleeve length (1 cm of shirt visible), jacket waist (a fist must pass easily) and jacket length (covers the buttocks). A good pair of shoes completes the look: the [men's derby shoes guide](/en/blog/best-mens-derby-shoes-guide/) details the selection criteria.

### Mistakes to avoid

1. Buying a suit too tight thinking it will loosen: a well-cut suit is comfortable from purchase
2. Neglecting alterations: an unaltered suit, even premium, will look ill-fitted
3. Prioritising brand over cut: a well-fitted IZAC cut outperforms a badly fitted Sandro cut
