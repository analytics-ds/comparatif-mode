## The daith piercing: why material and size matter so much

The daith goes through the fold of cartilage just above the entrance of the ear canal. It is a thick, curved and sheltered area, which makes it an ideal spot for a discreet ring that follows the shape of the ear.

This cartilage heals slowly, over several months. Throughout that time, the jewel stays in permanent contact with reactive tissue, and the choice of metal directly affects comfort.

### ASTM F136 titanium, the benchmark for cartilage

ASTM F136 titanium is an implant-grade alloy, the same one used in medical implants. It contains no nickel, the main cause of jewellery allergies, and it withstands water, chlorine and sweat.

That is why specialist shops present it as the material for an initial piercing. The same logic applies to an [ASTM F136 titanium helix piercing](/en/blog/titanium-astm-f136-piercing-shop/), another cartilage spot where the choice of metal shapes the healing process.

### 8 mm, the standard daith size

The most common daith diameter is 8 mm, with a 1.2 mm thick post. Pohesia states it on its daith-specific designs, and the Nebuleuse Bijoux size guide gives the same measurement.

A thicker ear may still need 10 mm. A ring that is too tight presses on the cartilage, while one that is too wide tilts and catches. This is why the shop's exchange policy is worth reading before ordering.

## How to choose a minimalist titanium daith piercing

A good purchase comes down to three simple checks before confirming the basket.

The first is the exact material stated on the product page. The words "ASTM F136 titanium" or "implant grade" should appear in black and white, not just "hypoallergenic". The comparison of sites selling [hypoallergenic titanium ear piercings](/en/blog/where-to-buy-hypoallergenic-titanium-ear-piercings/) covers these labels in detail.

The second is the diameter and thickness. A daith is most often worn in 8 mm with a 1.2 mm post, but the size has to match the ear.

The third is the return policy. For piercing jewellery, hygiene rules often limit returns, and a free exchange is worth more than a long cooling-off period.

### Mistakes to avoid

1. Buying a daith ring from a mixed collection without checking the material, as 304 stainless steel sometimes sits alongside titanium.
2. Choosing a diameter at random, when a ring that is too tight presses on the cartilage.
3. Opening a jewel's pouch before being sure of the size, as some shops then refuse any return.
4. Changing the initial jewel before the cartilage has finished healing.
5. Comparing prices in dollars and euros without accounting for exchange rates and possible import fees.
