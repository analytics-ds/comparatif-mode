The camel coat moves through trends without ever dating. This warm colour, between beige and caramel, pairs with almost anything and gives a polished look in one stroke. It is also an investment: a plain wool cut lasts ten years. You just have to know how to pair it, because badly matched it quickly turns dull. Here are the combinations that work.

## The colours that go with camel

Camel is a warm neutral, which makes it easy to pair but sensitive to taste slips. Four families show it off:

- **Cool neutrals**: white, grey, navy. The warm-cool contrast is clean and elegant, the safe bet.
- **Black**: a strong, almost graphic classic. Camel and black give a structured silhouette.
- **Other earthy tones**: brown, ecru, khaki, burgundy. A warm blend, softer, for a muted look.
- **A touch of bright colour**: a royal-blue or bottle-green jumper under the coat wakes the whole thing up.

What to avoid: colours too close that cancel each other out (a pale beige over camel goes muddy) and loud shades that clash with the warmth of the tone.

## In a casual outfit

Camel naturally softens a casual look. Raw jeans, a grey or navy crew-neck jumper, white trainers or brown leather boots: the coat dresses the whole thing without making it formal. It is the everyday outfit that looks thought through when it took no effort.

For winter, add a wool scarf in a tone that cuts through (charcoal, midnight blue) rather than a camel-on-camel that fades away.

## In a dressed outfit

Over a suit, the camel coat stylishly replaces the black or navy one. A grey or navy suit, a white shirt, leather shoes: camel brings warmth to an outfit that could be cold. For a faultless result, keep the rest plain and let the coat be the piece that draws the eye.

Length matters here. A coat that stops above the knee or at mid-thigh works on most silhouettes. Longer, you need height so it does not shorten you.

## The cut by body type

| Silhouette | Advised cut |
|---|---|
| Slim or tall | Straight or long cut, oversized possible |
| Broad frame | Straight cut, clean shoulders, avoid too fitted |
| Short height | Mid-thigh maximum, belt to mark the waist |

## Care and pitfalls

Camel wool shows stains and rain more than a dark colour. A spray waterproofer from the day you buy it protects the fibre. Brush the coat regularly with a clothes brush to lift dust and pills, and send it to the dry cleaner once or twice a season, no more: dry cleaning weakens wool over time.

The last pitfall, the most common: a camel coat badly fitted at the shoulders ruins everything else. That is the zone to check first when trying on, before even the length. A shoulder that drops too low or pulls gives away an unsuitable cut, and no accessory will rescue it.
