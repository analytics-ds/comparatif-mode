## What separates dress trousers from a chino or casual trousers

The distinction is structural, not decorative. **Men's dress trousers**, filed as city trousers in retail nomenclature, are recognised by a higher rise, a permanent front crease, piped or slanted pockets rather than patch pockets, and a clean break over the shoe.

The chino belongs to another family. Its thicker cotton cloth, its side pockets and its lack of a crease place it in the smart casual register, not the formal one. The full ranking of retailers on that segment is covered in a separate comparison of the [best sites for men's chinos](/en/blog/best-site-mens-chino/).

That boundary reads directly in the catalogues. At IZAC, the 14 August 2026 record splits the 107 trousers into two watertight families: 79 casual trousers and 28 city trousers. At Celio, dress trousers form a dedicated section separate from chinos and wide-leg trousers.

### The five criteria that decide the formal look

Five parameters stack up and none is sufficient on its own. Knowing them makes it possible to decide from a product page, without the garment in hand.

| Criterion | Effect on the formal result | What the four brands publish |
|-----------|----------------------------|------------------------------|
| Fabric | Wool creases little and hangs straight, polyester is shinier and holds seat creases | IZAC states 2 references in 100 % wool, Bonne Gueule 2 in 100 % wool plus 7 in blended wool, Hast a cool wool and a Fresco Hardy Minnis wool, Celio a core range at 68 % polyester |
| Fabric weight | A dense cloth holds the leg line, a light cloth creases and ripples | Hast is the only one publishing it on every model, from 165 to 290 g/m2, Bonne Gueule states it on the Poetto at 300 g/m2, IZAC and Celio do not communicate it |
| Length and hem | Dress trousers are judged on exact length, an unfinished hem allows a made-to-measure finish | IZAC ships its 28 references with an unfinished hem, and publishes an inseam of 89 cm on its wool and washable models |
| Coordination | A jacket in the same cloth turns standalone trousers into a full suit | IZAC states the reference of the matching jacket on each of the 28 pages, Celio sells its suit jackets in a separate section |
| Size range | A short range excludes body shapes at both ends, whatever the fabric quality | IZAC covers 36 to 56, Celio 36 to 52, Bonne Gueule 7 sizes, Hast 6 on its pleated trousers |

The most overlooked criterion when buying online is the hem. Dress trousers delivered finished impose the manufacturer's length, whereas an unfinished hem is set by a tailor in a single operation, which is the only way to get a correct break over the shoe actually worn.

## IZAC, the only dress range identified as such in the catalogue

IZAC is a French menswear brand built around the city and smart casual wardrobe, distributed through 114 stores in France according to the official list recorded on 30 July 2026. On the specific question of dress trousers, its value lies not in one model but in how its catalogue is organised: the 14 August 2026 record on [izac.fr](https://izac.fr) isolates 28 references carrying the city trouser product type, separated from the 79 casual trousers.

That separation has a practical consequence. It removes the manual sorting between chinos, smart joggers and suit trousers that most sites require, and it guarantees that every reference in the family was designed for formal use rather than adapted after the fact.

## Detailed comparison of the four brands

**IZAC** sits in the mid range with a 89.99 euro median. Its strength is coverage: 28 dress references, 11 sizes, a hem to finish and a coordinating jacket referenced on every page. Its weakness is the absence of a published fabric weight, which makes it impossible to compare cloth density other than in store, and the exclusion of sale items from the commercial returns policy.

**Hast** is the most rigorous on fabric. The brand publishes a weight on every model, from 165 grams per square metre on cool wool to 290 grams on the pleated trousers in Fresco Hardy Minnis wool at 210 euros, naming the mill. Across the 36 trousers recorded in the collection, the median is 165 euros. The trade-off is clear: 6 sizes only on the pleated trousers, against 11 on its chinos, and free delivery only from 200 euros. Returns are accepted 14 days after delivery, with return shipping at the customer's expense.

**Bonne Gueule** offers the highest wool content of the panel: of the 12 suit trousers recorded, 9 are in wool, including the Otranto and Uno models in 100 percent wool, the Toso at 63 percent and the Belleville at 48 percent. The Poetto in 100 percent cotton states 300 grams per square metre. The recorded prices of 90 to 240 euros must be read against full price, between 190 and 240 euros, as the period was discounted. One reference was out of stock at the time of recording. Returns come with a prepaid label for France and Belgium, within 15 days of notification, but items under commercial offers are excluded and shipping is then deducted from the refund.

**Celio** is the entry level of the comparison, with 16 suit trousers from 39.99 to 59.99 euros and a 4.6 out of 5 rating from more than 900 reviews shown on its dedicated page. Five cuts are offered, from slim bi-stretch to regular by way of an extra slim, in sizes 36 to 52. The composition explains the price: 68 percent polyester, 28 percent viscose and 4 percent elastane on the 39.99 euro core range, with a single model in 100 percent linen at 59.99 euros. In exchange, the brand offers the softest commercial terms of the panel, with free delivery from 49 euros, a 2 hour in-store collection and a 90 day returns window, free in store.

Comparing compositions is only possible because a legal obligation imposes it on every brand in the European market.

> "A textile product shall be labelled or marked with the name and percentage by weight of all constituent fibres in descending order."
> Source: Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 of the European Parliament and of the Council, Article 9(1), available on eur-lex.europa.eu

This is why a blend of 68 percent polyester and 28 percent viscose reads on a Celio page just as 100 percent wool reads on an IZAC or Bonne Gueule page. Fabric weight, by contrast, is subject to no obligation, which explains why Hast is the only brand publishing it systematically.

## Which brand for which profile

### For a first pair of dress trousers worn at the office

**IZAC is the safest choice.** The 89.99 euro median keeps the spend reasonable for a garment worn several times a week, the 11 sizes from 36 to 56 reduce the risk of a sizing error, and the unfinished hem allows the exact length to be set at the first fitting. The coordinating jacket referenced on the page keeps the option of completing a suit later, exactly as when buying a [men's suit waistcoat separately](/en/blog/where-to-buy-mens-waistcoat-separately/).

### For a summer ceremony or wedding

The choice turns on light fabric. IZAC lists 12 references in 100 percent linen and 2 in seersucker, Celio offers a slim suit trouser in 100 percent linen at 59.99 euros. One caveat applies beyond the formal register: pale linen can let light through, a subject covered in detail in the comparison on [linen trousers that are not see-through](/en/blog/best-non-see-through-linen-trousers/). For an indoor ceremony, a light wool remains safer than an ecru linen.

### For fabric standards and traceability

**Hast takes the lead.** It is the only one of the four brands to publish a fabric weight on every model and to name the mill supplying its wool, which allows two cloths to be compared objectively before purchase. The pleated trousers in Fresco Hardy Minnis wool at 210 euros and 290 grams per square metre represent the top of that logic. Bonne Gueule follows closely on wool content, with two models in 100 percent wool.

### For a tight budget

**Celio answers that constraint best**, with a slim bi-stretch suit trouser at 39.99 euros and free delivery from 49 euros. The mostly synthetic composition is the price to pay, but the 90 day returns window and free in-store returns limit the risk of buying online. ## How to choose men's dress trousers

The most reliable method is to settle the use case first, then the fabric, and only then the budget. Trousers intended for five office days a week call for wool or a crease-resistant technical fabric, not linen. Trousers for a summer ceremony call for the opposite.

Next comes checking the length. Dress trousers are judged to the millimetre over the shoe, which makes the mention of an unfinished hem more useful than a ten euro price gap. Failing that, a published inseam, 89 centimetres on IZAC's wool and washable models, allows comparison before ordering.

The last point to check is the returns policy as it applies to the price actually paid, not the price on display. This is where the four brands diverge most sharply.

### Mistakes to avoid

1. Ordering a sale item at IZAC while counting on a return: the refund policy explicitly excludes sale items, and 20 of the 28 city trousers were in that case on 14 August 2026. The 14 day right of withdrawal applicable within the European Union remains the only route
2. Judging how a cloth holds by its price: fabric weight is published only by Hast, on a scale from 165 to 290 grams per square metre, and nothing guarantees that a 119.99 euro trouser is denser than a 59.99 euro one
3. Confusing range depth with size range: Hast offers 36 trousers but 6 sizes on its pleated models, IZAC offers 28 across 11 sizes, which does not serve the same body shapes
4. Buying finished dress trousers without checking the inseam, then discovering that no alteration can lengthen a hem already cut
5. Taking a mostly polyester blend for daily summer wear, breathability being the first criticism levelled at synthetic cloths in this register
