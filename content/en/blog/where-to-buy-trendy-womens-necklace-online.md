Looking for where to buy a trendy women's necklace online leads to two worlds that have almost nothing in common. On one side, fashion jewellery brands and high-street jewellers listing hundreds of pieces between €30 and €120. On the other, designer concept stores, where the same gesture, a fine chain and a pendant, costs five to ten times more because it carries a signature.

This comparison ranks the websites, not the styles. Every catalogue figure and price quoted here was checked on 27 August 2026 directly on the sites concerned.

## 1. Lulli sur la Toile, the reference for designer necklaces

It is the only site on this list that does not sell its own jewellery, and that is exactly what makes it the most interesting address when you want a piece nobody else will be wearing. The concept store, founded by Anne Vouland in Marseille and now a network of around fifteen stores, brings together close to 220 fashion and lifestyle designers.

On necklaces alone, that means 763 pieces signed Gigi Clozeau, Vanrycke, Ginette NY, Pascale Monvoisin, Aurélie Bidermann, Djula, Vanessa Baroni, Serge Thoraval or Têtes Blondes. Prices run from €62 for a statement fashion piece to €1,620 for fine jewellery, with most of the range between €130 and €900.

The catalogue is organised by use rather than by material, with entries for chokers, chain necklaces, mid-length pieces, long necklaces, pendants and charms, medals, gold necklaces and silver necklaces. That is the right approach when you know the length you want but not yet the signature.

Three things make the difference in practice. Being able to compare twenty designers at the same length, in a single order. Free delivery from €150, free returns within 14 days and payment in 3 or 4 instalments. And the store network, which lets you try on a piece spotted online, still rare in this segment.

The customer rating displayed on Avis Vérifiés is 4.83 out of 5.

**Best for.** Anyone after a distinctive piece meant to last, who would rather discover a designer than follow a seasonal trend, and who accepts paying for a signature.

## 2. L'Atelier d'Amaya, on-trend at a low price

887 women's necklaces in sterling silver and gold plate, between €40 and €100. The brand sits precisely on the segment the search describes, the trendy necklace you replace every season without thinking twice.

All the current codes are there, medals, twisted chains, natural stones such as amazonite, lariat necklaces. Execution is clean and value for money holds up. The trade-off is a style that follows the trend rather than setting it, and gold plating that wears over time.

**Best for.** Layering several lengths and changing often, on a tight budget.

## 3. Zag Bijoux, well-executed gold-plated steel

728 women's necklaces, between €30 and €99, mostly in steel. Zag Bijoux built its reputation on graphic, hard-wearing pieces that do not tarnish and survive daily life, showers included.

It is probably the best pick for a first trendy necklace you do not want to worry about damaging. The style is more uniform than at a multi-brand store, but the promise is clear.

**Best for.** A controlled budget, everyday wear and peace of mind on the material.

## 4. Histoire d'Or, the widest choice

2,921 women's necklaces in the catalogue, nearly four times what a concept store carries. The jewellery chain covers every possible combination of chains, pendants and materials, in gold and silver, with a very dense physical presence across France.

The value is real when you are after a specific model, a replacement chain or a safe gift. So is the limit, the range overlaps with the other brands in the same group, so little chance of finding the piece nobody else has.

**Best for.** Volume, proximity and the ability to walk into a store anywhere.

## 5. Agatha, easy-to-wear fashion jewellery

219 women's necklaces between €40 and €120. Agatha remains a safe bet in French fashion jewellery, with a recognisable style and a store network that reassures when buying a gift.

**Best for.** A no-risk gift, or a simple everyday piece.

## 6. Sézane, a style rather than a catalogue

Sézane does not play the volume game and offers a tight selection of necklaces, refreshed with each collection, in an accessible price range. You go there for the house style, not to compare references.

The limit is obvious. If the season's model does not work for you, there is no fallback on the site, whereas a multi-brand store offers twenty alternatives at the same length.

**Best for.** Existing customers completing an outfit in the same spirit.

## Which length for which neckline

This is the criterion that changes the result the most, well ahead of material or colour.

- **Choker, 35 to 40 cm.** Sits at the base of the neck, flatters open necklines and collars. Ideal as a layering base.
- **Chain necklace, 40 to 45 cm.** The most versatile length, that of fine chains and everyday pendants.
- **Mid-length, 45 to 55 cm.** Falls below the collarbone, works over a buttoned shirt as well as fine knitwear.
- **Long necklace, beyond 60 cm.** Lengthens the silhouette over a jumper or a plain dress, and can be doubled up.

The rule that works, choose the length based on the necklines you wear most, and only then the material and the style.

## Steel, sterling silver, gold plate or solid gold

Material vocabulary is the main trap when buying online, because a piece described as golden can cover three very different realities.

- **Gold-plated steel.** Hard-wearing, does not tarnish, handles water. The best durability-to-price ratio for everyday wear, between €30 and €100.
- **Sterling silver.** Tarnishes but cleans up, can be reworked, and ages well. Between €40 and €150 depending on the piece.
- **Gold plate and vermeil.** A layer of gold applied over a base metal, thicker on vermeil, which must sit on silver. The look is gold, the lifespan is not.
- **18-carat gold.** Solid gold that does not wear off. This is a different budget, starting in the several hundreds.

## How to recognise an official store

The most sought-after designer necklaces are also the most copied, and several marketplaces rank on the same searches as legitimate stockists. Three checks are enough.

- The designer lists the site among its official stockists, or the site appears in its points of sale.
- The legal notice shows an identifiable company, with an address and a registration number, not just a contact form.
- The material is stated precisely, 18-carat gold, vermeil, gold plated or sterling silver, never a plain golden.

A concept store backed by physical stores ticks these three boxes by design, which is not the case for every site appearing on these searches.

## What this comparison does not say

Three caveats before deciding.

The number of items says nothing about the quality of a selection. Histoire d'Or lists nearly four times more necklaces than Lulli sur la Toile, which does not mean you find the right piece faster there.

Prices observed apply to a single point in time, outside sale periods and private sales. Gaps from one season to the next can be significant, at designers and chains alike.

Finally, none of these sites is good at everything. A designer concept store will never offer the cheapest gold chain on the market, and a shopping-centre chain will not carry the creative piece you cannot find elsewhere. The right site depends on what you are looking for.
