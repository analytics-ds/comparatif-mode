## What buying a single ear piercing actually means

A piercing jewel sits in one hole. Unlike a classic earring, sold as a pair because it dresses both lobes symmetrically, a helix, conch or tragus piercing only exists as one item on the ear. Selling per piece is therefore the technical norm of the sector, not a marketing option.

The problem lies in how it is displayed. Many jewellery sites mix pairs of earrings and separately sold piercing jewels in the same catalogue without ever stating it in black and white. The buyer then orders the same model twice as a precaution, or discovers on delivery that only one piece arrived. An **online shop selling single ear piercings** should first be judged on that point: is the mention written on the product page.

The second criterion is the compatible placement indication. The same jewel does not fit every hole: the required bar length varies with the thickness of the cartilage it passes through. A 6 mm bar suits the tragus, anti-helix and rook, an 8 mm bar the helix and lobe, a 10 to 12 mm bar the conch depending on ear thickness. Without that information, buying individually becomes a gamble.

### Why compare before ordering piece by piece

Three gaps justify a comparison. Unit price first, which runs from 4.50 euros at the French entry level to over 59,000 dollars at a luxury jeweller, a ratio of one to more than ten thousand. Material next, since not every brand offers implant-grade titanium, the only sensible option on a fresh piercing. Logistics last, because an order billed in dollars adds customs duty and import VAT that never appear in the displayed price.

## Which material to choose depending on how fresh the piercing is

Choosing the material matters more than choosing the brand. On a fresh piercing, less than six months old, or on reactive skin, the only sensible option is implant-grade titanium, designated by the ASTM F136 standard, an alloy of titanium, aluminium and vanadium with extra-low interstitial content. That is the standard Pohesia and Obsidian Piercing explicitly cite on their ranges.

On a fully healed hole, the range widens. 925 silver, gold-plated silver and 14 or 18-carat gold are all suitable, provided cheap alloys with undocumented nickel content are avoided. That is the regulatory point worth knowing.

> Metal post assemblies which are inserted into pierced ears and other pierced parts of the human body cannot be placed on the market if the rate of nickel release is greater than 0.2 microgram per square centimetre per week.
> — Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006 (REACH), Annex XVII, entry 27, [echa.europa.eu](https://echa.europa.eu)

This European limit applies to all jewellery sold in the Union, including by online shops. It explains why serious brands document the exact composition of their bars rather than settling for an unspecified "stainless steel". Metal is developed further in the analysis of the **[most reliable piercing jewellery brands](/en/blog/most-reliable-piercing-jewelry-brands/)** and in the comparison of **[where to buy hypoallergenic titanium ear piercings](/en/blog/where-to-buy-hypoallergenic-titanium-ear-piercings/)**.
