## Why linen has become a summer wardrobe staple for men

Linen is the most sought-after natural fibre for summer thanks to its thermoregulating properties and breathability superior to cotton. A **men's linen shirt** absorbs up to 20 percent of its weight in moisture before feeling wet, making it the ideal fabric in high heat. France is also the world's leading linen producer, with roughly 75 percent of global production grown between Normandy and northern Europe, which guarantees strong traceability on certified models.

### Criteria for choosing a men's linen shirt

- **Linen type**: classic pure linen for a dressier finish, washed (stonewashed) linen for a soft drape and a deliberate relaxed crinkle
- **Fabric weight**: between 140 and 170 gsm for a shirt; below 120 gsm the fabric becomes sheer and fragile
- **Origin**: European linen certified European Flax or Masters of Linen guarantees rain-fed cultivation and full traceability
- **Fit**: slim for slimmer builds, regular straight for most body types, relaxed for a casual summer style

## Mistakes to avoid when buying a men's linen shirt

1. **Confusing pure linen and blends**: check the composition label; a blend under 55 percent linen loses the thermoregulating properties of pure linen
2. **Ignoring the weight**: linen under 120 gsm becomes sheer and tears at the seams after a few seasons
3. **Misjudging the crinkle**: linen creases by nature; choose washed linen if the relaxed crinkle bothers you, or accept this character on classic pure linen
4. **Neglecting care**: wash at 30 degrees, dry flat or on a hanger, iron slightly damp to preserve the fibre
5. **Overpaying for the brand**: an IZAC at 110 euros offers 150 gsm pure linen equivalent to a 120% Lino at 180 euros, the price gap coming mainly from piece dyeing and the name

<summary>How do you care for a men's linen shirt?</summary>

Wash linen at 30 degrees maximum on a delicate cycle, avoid the tumble dryer which weakens the fibre, and favour drying flat or on a hanger. Ironing is done on slightly damp linen at the linen temperature setting (around 210 degrees). A quality men's linen shirt (IZAC, Hast, 120% Lino) lasts 6 to 12 years with this care, compared with 4 to 6 years for a lighter linen such as Uniqlo's.
