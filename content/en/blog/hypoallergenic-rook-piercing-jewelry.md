## Why a rook piercing gets irritated: the causes to know

The rook passes through a poorly vascularized area of cartilage located in the fold above the tragus. This low blood supply explains a long healing time, 6 to 12 months, compared with 6 to 8 weeks for a simple lobe.

Throughout this period, the tissue stays reactive to its environment. Choosing **hypoallergenic rook piercing jewelry** then becomes the first variable to act on to limit irritation.

### Nickel, the main cause of allergy

Nickel is the leading cause of contact dermatitis linked to jewelry. An estimated 10 to 20% of the population is sensitive to it, with a higher prevalence among women. Jewelry that releases nickel causes redness, itching and oozing around the rook.

The European REACH regulation limits this release for items in prolonged contact with the skin: it must not exceed 0.5 micrograms per cm² per week. Genuinely **hypoallergenic** jewelry stays well below this threshold, ideally at zero nickel. This is also why items sold as [nickel-free ear piercings](/en/blog/nickel-free-ear-piercings/) are favored from the moment of piercing.

### Friction and a long healing time

The rook's position exposes it to friction from hair, clothing and above all the pillow. Sleeping on the pierced side creates constant pressure that sustains inflammation and can encourage a keloid.

Jewelry that is the wrong size or shape worsens this friction. A slightly longer straight barbell is often preferred during healing to accommodate swelling without compressing the tissue.

### Titanium ASTM F136: the reference

Titanium ASTM F136, also called grade 23 or G23, is the alloy recommended by most piercers for a fresh piercing. Nickel-free, light and fully biocompatible, it matches the standard used for surgical implants.

It is the material to favor for a healing or already irritated rook. It is found at retailers selling [hypoallergenic titanium ear piercings](/en/blog/where-to-buy-hypoallergenic-titanium-ear-piercings/), including Pohésia from 25 euros.

### 18-carat gold

Solid 18-carat gold, meaning 750 parts per thousand, is hypoallergenic provided it is solid and not plated. Its alloy can still contain traces of other metals, so it is reserved for fully healed piercings.

On a freshly pierced or reactive rook, it is better to wait until healing is complete before switching to gold. Surface gold plating does not offer the same safety as solid gold.

### Surgical steel 316L and flexible materials

Surgical steel 316L contains traces of nickel, generally below the REACH threshold, which is nonetheless enough to trigger a reaction in very sensitive people. It suits tolerant skin and a healed piercing, not a rook that is already irritated.

Bioplast and PTFE are flexible, metal-free polymers, useful in cases of severe allergy or strong inflammation. Their flexibility limits friction, but they remain transitional, used to calm an irritation before switching back to titanium.
