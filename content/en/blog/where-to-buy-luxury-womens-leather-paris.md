Buying **luxury leather in Paris** raises a problem other cities do not have, there are too many addresses and they do not do the same thing. A specialist house, a department store and a leather retailer offer neither the same advice, nor the same choice, nor the same service. Here is the map by type of address, with what you actually find at each.

## In short

1. Three types of address coexist in Paris, houses **specialising in leather garments**, **department stores** and more accessible **leather retailers**.
2. The **8th arrondissement**, around the Faubourg Saint-Honoré, concentrates the luxury houses and the leather specialists.
3. **Boulevard Haussmann** remains the best place to try several brands in a single visit.
4. **Made-to-measure leather** is rare in Paris. When a house offers it, that is the most reliable sign it genuinely has a workshop.
5. In store, the test that settles everything is **trying the piece on with your arms raised**. A hide that is too rigid restricts the shoulders whatever the price tag says.

These five rows do not compete, they answer different buying moments. You compare in a department store, you buy a wardrobe staple at a specialist.

## Houses specialising in leather garments

This is the least known and most useful category when looking for a piece that will last, because these houses do nothing else.

**Jitrois** is the Paris reference for couture leather. The house, founded by Jean-Claude Jitrois, inventor of stretch leather, keeps a single boutique at **38 rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré in the 8th**, open Monday to Saturday from 11am to 7pm. It carries women's and men's ready-to-wear plus accessories, and above all **made-to-measure**, available in store and remotely.

The point of going in person for this kind of piece is concrete. Leather cannot be fixed after purchase, it keeps the mark of every stitch and cannot be altered like fabric. Seeing the hide, folding it, trying it on with your arms raised changes the decision entirely. It is also the only place to start a made-to-measure request on a length or a shoulder width.

**Yves Salomon**, at 245 rue Saint-Honoré, works a wardrobe more oriented towards fur and long pieces. **Ventcouvert** is the other Paris name in designer leather.

## Department stores, for comparing widely

**Galeries Lafayette** at 40 boulevard Haussmann and **Le Printemps** at 64 boulevard Haussmann bring together most contemporary brands and part of the luxury offer in one place. It is by far the fastest way to try five or six leather jackets from different brands in the same afternoon.

**Le Bon Marché**, at 24 rue de Sèvres in the 7th, offers a more curated selection, with a sharper designer choice and less volume.

The limit of these addresses is advice. Staff know the brands present but rarely the hides in detail, and made-to-measure does not exist.

## Leather retailers, for comparing hides

**Cuir-City**, **DKS Cuir** and **Lattitude cuir** occupy a different segment, that of choice and price. You find many models, varied hides and more accessible budgets.

This is the right place for a first leather purchase, or to train your eye by comparing hides side by side before moving up elsewhere. Corrected grain is common there, however, so look closely at the type of leather stated.

## Where to buy an elegant women's leather jacket in Paris

On jackets, the word elegant makes all the difference, because it rules out the biker register that dominates the offer.

For a **dressy rather than biker** jacket, the Faubourg Saint-Honoré and rue Saint-Honoré are the right streets, with the leather garment houses. The boulevard Haussmann department stores cover contemporary, with Sandro, Maje, Zadig & Voltaire and The Kooples all present between roughly 400 and 900 euros.

The test to run systematically in the fitting room is **trying it on with your arms raised**. A rigid leather jacket locks the shoulders and rides up as one piece, which is why heritage cuts are wide. A stretch hide allows a close cut without that constraint. On a piece worn all day, the gap is decisive.

## Where to buy a high-end long women's leather coat in Paris

The long coat is harder to find than the jacket, because few houses make one. The weight of leather concentrates at the shoulders in a long format, which demands real construction work.

**Specialist houses** are therefore the first move. Jitrois offers a long-length range at 38 rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré, with the Jagger, Julian, Laya, Manicui and Trinity models, plus a Melina trench line available in leather and suede. Yves Salomon also works the long wardrobe.

**Department stores** give access to generalist luxury houses in this format, Saint Laurent or Loewe for example, but the long leather coat offer there stays seasonal and often limited to one or two models.

Two things to check in store. The **actual length**, below the knee being the threshold for winter, and the **lining**, which should be sewn and not glued, including in the sleeves. Brand detail is in our comparison of the [best women's leather coat brands](/en/blog/best-womens-leather-coat-brands/).

## Our advice before going in store

**Know what you are looking for before you leave.** A jacket, a long coat and a tailored leather jacket are not found at the same addresses. Setting out without deciding wastes the afternoon.

**Ask for the type of hide, not the material.** A serious house states full grain leather. If the answer stops at genuine leather, there is often corrected grain behind it. Our criteria are detailed in the guide on [how to identify a quality leather garment](/en/blog/how-to-identify-quality-leather-garment/).

**Try things on late in the day.** Leather relaxes slightly with body heat. Trying a piece cold first thing in the morning feels stiffer than reality.

**Plan ahead for made-to-measure.** If you want an adjustment, allow several weeks and expect a first visit for measurements.
