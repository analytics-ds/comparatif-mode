Searching for designer shoes online comes with a letdown. The query mostly returns budget footwear sites that sell volume rather than design, where the word designer works as a sales argument more than a reality.

This comparison sets those sites aside. It covers the addresses where you actually find signed shoes, pumps, boots, loafers, sandals or cowboy boots carrying the name of an identifiable designer. Every figure quoted was checked on 27 August 2026 directly on the sites concerned.

## 1. Lulli sur la Toile, the best balance between design and budget

The concept store, founded by Anne Vouland in Marseille and now a network of around fifteen stores, brings together close to 220 fashion and lifestyle designers. On footwear, that means **2,160 items**.

What sets it apart is where it sits on the price scale. Shoes run from **€100 to €545**, whereas luxury marketplaces start considerably higher. You will find Toral and K.Jacques for Mediterranean craftsmanship, Senso on pumps and heeled ankle boots, Isabel Marant, Golden Goose, Autry, Mercer Amsterdam, Blonde No.8, Destin or Margaux Lönnberg.

The catalogue is organised by shape rather than by designer, with entries for ballet flats, loafers, mules, sandals, heels, clogs, ankle boots, boots and cowboy boots. That is the right approach when you are after a specific shape for a season rather than a particular signature.

Free delivery from €150, free returns within 14 days, payment in 3 or 4 instalments, a customer rating of 4.83 out of 5 on Avis Vérifiés. And above all the store network, which lets you try shoes on, decisive on a category where sizing is unforgiving.

**Best for.** Anyone after a signed pair they will genuinely wear, between €150 and €500, who would rather try before committing.

## 2. Jane de Boy, the tightest selection

**182 women's designer shoes**, between **€99 and €400**. Jane de Boy takes the opposite approach to the catalogue, a short and heavily curated selection where every model has been chosen.

That is comfortable when you do not know exactly what you want and accept being guided. It is limiting as soon as you have a precise shape in mind, since the choice within each category stays narrow.

**Best for.** Anyone who would rather be carried by a selection than filter a catalogue.

## 3. Farfetch, maximum choice on luxury

Farfetch aggregates the stock of hundreds of boutiques and houses worldwide, making it the most complete address in this comparison on the luxury segment. A model still available somewhere in a rare size stands a good chance of being there.

The trade-off comes with the marketplace model. Orders ship from different sellers, with delivery times and returns conditions that vary from one order to the next. And pricing is that of the luxury houses.

**Best for.** A specific model from a major house, or a size you cannot find elsewhere.

## 4. Mytheresa, a stylist's eye on the high end

Mytheresa plays selection over exhaustiveness, with careful editorial work and a focus on the season's pieces from the major houses. Pricing remains luxury.

**Best for.** Anyone buying established houses who wants guidance on new arrivals.

## 5. 24S, the Bon Marché spirit online

24S extends the Paris department store's selection online, with the point of view that comes with it. You go there for a certain idea of French elegance, not to compare references.

**Best for.** Anyone who already recognises the house style.

## 6. Printemps, the department store reflex

The Printemps luxury and designer floor covers women's footwear with department store logic, established houses and a service backed by the physical network.

**Best for.** Anyone who wants the option of walking into a store.

## Pump, loafer, boot, what changes from one site to the next

The number of items says nothing until you look at the shape you are after. That is where the gaps show.

- **Pumps and heeled sandals.** The territory of the luxury houses, so of the marketplaces. A concept store carries them, but at more wearable heights.
- **Loafers and ballet flats.** The most contested segment, where mid-sized designers are best placed. Toral and K.Jacques are safe bets.
- **Boots and cowboy boots.** A seasonal investment, €300 to €600 at designers, well beyond that at the major houses.
- **Flat sandals.** Craftsmanship matters more than the name, and K.Jacques, made in Saint-Tropez, remains the reference.

## How to recognise an official stockist

Designer footwear is heavily copied, and several sites rank on these searches without being distributors. Three checks are enough.

- The designer lists the site among its official stockists, or the site appears in its points of sale.
- The legal notice shows an identifiable company, with an address and a registration number.
- The site states the material and the place of manufacture, not just a mention of leather.

## What this comparison does not say

A large catalogue is no guarantee. A site aggregating thousands of pairs can be harder to work through than a selection of two hundred well-chosen models.

Prices observed apply to a single point in time, outside sale periods and private sales, and gaps from one season to the next are significant in this segment.

Finally, none of these sites covers everything. A designer concept store will not carry a major house's couture pump, and a luxury marketplace will not carry the €180 artisanal sandal. The right site depends on the pair you are looking for.
