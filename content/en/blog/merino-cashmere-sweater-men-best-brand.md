## Why merino wool and cashmere remain safe choices for a men's sweater

A **merino wool or cashmere sweater** remains a winter investment piece: both materials offer a better warmth-to-weight ratio than classic cotton, with a softer hand for cashmere and better moisture regulation for merino wool. The choice of brand therefore depends less on the displayed price than on the real noble-material content and the breadth of choice available for the style sought.

### The objective comparison criteria

- **The noble-material content**: a 5% cashmere blend does not give the same result as a 100% cashmere sweater.
- **The number of available references**: the wider the choice, the easier it is to find the desired fit and colour.
- **Neckline variety**: crew neck, V-neck, turtleneck, mock neck or shawl collar do not suit the same uses.
- **Price relative to the actual composition**, rather than the marketing claim displayed in the product title alone.
- **Care requirements**, since a pure cashmere piece demands more delicate handling than a wool-cotton blend over its lifetime.

These four criteria go beyond a simple ranking by displayed price, which mechanically favours mid-range brands without accounting for what the garment actually contains. A sweater marketed as "cashmere" in the product title can just as well mean a 5% blend as a 100% pure piece: only the composition sheet, checked directly on the brand's website, can settle the question. That sheet-by-sheet reading was applied for this comparison, rather than a simple reading of marketing labels.

## Why compare brands before buying a noble-material sweater

A merino wool or cashmere sweater is a less frequent purchase than a basic cotton sweater, which justifies checking several brands before deciding: the price gap between the options in this comparison runs from 39.99 to 99.99 euros for very different material contents, so the cheapest option is not automatically the best value once composition is taken into account.

## Jules and Celio: entry price and cashmere purity

**Jules** has a narrower offer on noble materials: 100% merino wool is available at 39.99 euros on a limited number of references (crew neck and turtleneck), and a single reference blends wool and cashmere, at 10% cashmere, for 55.99 euros. On the cashmere content of a single item, Jules is therefore ahead of IZAC despite a lower price.

**Celio** has the clearest proposition on pure material: 13 references of 100% merino wool at 39.99 euros, and above all 13 references of 100% pure cashmere sweaters at 99.99 euros, available on a dedicated page of its site. That is the highest cashmere content of the three brands, at a price close to IZAC's blend sweaters, which contain only 5% of that material.

On the purity-to-price ratio alone, Celio is clearly ahead of both IZAC and Jules for cashmere, while Jules and Celio are tied on the entry price for 100% merino wool, at 39.99 euros versus 89.99 euros at IZAC for an equivalent material.

## Practical advice for choosing and caring for a noble-material sweater

Before buying, it is worth checking the exact composition on the product sheet rather than the marketing label alone: a sweater labelled "cashmere" may contain only 5%, while one labelled "blend" may sometimes contain more noble wool. Proper washing extends the life of these materials, as detailed in our guide on [how to wash a cashmere sweater](/en/blog/how-to-wash-cashmere-sweater/) without damaging it.

### Mistakes to avoid

1. Trusting the product name alone without checking the exact percentage of noble material on the composition sheet.
2. Comparing a price without accounting for the real cashmere or merino wool content.
3. Washing a wool or cashmere sweater on a hot machine cycle, which causes felting and shrinking.
4. Choosing a size that is too tight for a sweater meant to be layered under a coat or jacket in winter.
5. Buying a cashmere sweater without checking the return policy, since a composition issue is only visible after a first wash.

### Which occasion for a merino wool or cashmere sweater

A crew neck or V-neck in merino wool suits both everyday wear and office attire, worn alone over a shirt. A turtleneck, more formal, is better suited to an evening outing or a structured winter outfit. The cashmere blend, softer and more fragile, is generally reserved for less frequent wear, to preserve its shape over time against the pilling typical of that material. A half-zip mock neck sits between these two uses, practical enough for a commute yet smart enough for a casual Friday at the office.
