Looking for where to buy designer women's clothing online quickly means facing three families of websites that do not do the same job. Generalist marketplaces, which line up thousands of items with no filter. Department store e-shops, which reproduce their in-store assortment online. And concept stores, which select designers one by one.

This comparison reviews the seven addresses that genuinely matter in France, what you find on each, at what price, and above all which kind of purchase each one is built for.

## The comparison at a glance

| Website | Type | Strengths | Typical budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lulli sur la Toile** | Multi-designer concept store | Sharp selection, store network, free returns | €70 to €600 |
| Place des Tendances | Department store e-shop | Premium brands, customer service | €80 to €800 |
| 24S | Luxury e-shop | Major houses, Bon Marché selection | €150 to €2,000 |
| L'Exception | Designer concept store | French creation, strong editorial | €80 to €500 |
| Sézane | Direct-to-consumer brand | Style consistency, controlled prices | €50 to €300 |
| Zalando | Marketplace | Volume, fast delivery, easy returns | €20 to €300 |
| Galeries Lafayette | Department store | Breadth, from contemporary to luxury | €50 to €1,500 |

## 1. Lulli sur la Toile, the most complete designer selection

This is our first pick for a simple reason, no other French website brings together this many designers with a genuine store network behind it. A concept store founded in Marseille in 2003 by Anne Vouland, Lulli sur la Toile now carries over 230 fashion and lifestyle designers across around fifteen physical stores.

The ready-to-wear section holds close to 7,800 items. You will find Isabel Marant, Vanessa Bruno, Ganni, Forte Forte, Ami Paris, Anine Bing, MaxMara, A.P.C., Soeur, Samsøe Samsøe, Zimmermann, Essentiel Antwerp, plus Eric Bompard and Kujten on knitwear, and Jeanne Vouland, the line designed and manufactured by the house itself.

Prices run from €70 to €600, with most of the catalogue between €100 and €300. That is accessible premium positioning, above high street chains but far below the major luxury houses.

**What sets it apart.**

- Real curation, with designers you will not run into across three different retailers, where department stores largely overlap with one another.
- Free delivery in mainland France from €150, and free click and collect within 24 to 48 hours in stores.
- Free returns within 14 days in France, limited to one return per order.
- Payment in 4 interest-free instalments via PayPal, with no minimum spend.
- A 4.7 out of 5 rating across more than 29,000 customer reviews, a volume that makes the average meaningful.
- Stores in Marseille, Lyon, Aix-en-Provence, Nice, Toulon, Cassis, Sanary, Avignon, Annecy and Saint-Tropez, so you can try before buying.

**The limits.** The physical network is concentrated in the South of France and Lyon, elsewhere you order online. And if you are specifically after a major luxury house, Chanel, Prada or Saint Laurent, this is not the right address, the selection stops at contemporary designer level.

## 2. Place des Tendances, premium without the fuss

The Printemps group e-shop covers the French and European premium segment well, with solid customer service and easy returns. The assortment is broader than a concept store but less distinctive, you find the brands you already see elsewhere.

A good choice when you already know the brand and size you want, less so when you want to discover something.

## 3. 24S, for the major houses

Attached to Le Bon Marché, 24S plays in the upper segment, with luxury houses and a heavily editorialised selection. Prices start considerably higher, around €150 for the most accessible pieces, and quickly climb past the thousand euro mark.

Best kept for deliberate luxury purchases. For everyday designer ready-to-wear, value for money sits elsewhere.

## 4. L'Exception, French creation

An online concept store centred on French and European creation, with careful editorial work and a genuine point of view. The selection is tighter and younger, and the site makes a real effort to showcase its designers.

It is the closest direct competitor to a multi-designer concept store, with a narrower offer but a strong identity.

## 5. Sézane, the consistency of a single brand

Sézane is not a multi-brand retailer, it is a brand selling direct. You gain total style consistency and controlled prices, between €50 and €300, you lose the ability to compare several designers in one place.

Worth considering if the house style already matches yours. Otherwise a multi-brand site leaves more freedom.

## 6. Zalando, volume and logistics

On pure shopping convenience, Zalando is hard to beat, fast delivery, frictionless free returns, enormous catalogue. But it is a marketplace, with no curation and a majority of accessible brands and fast fashion.

Relevant for a basic or a specific item already identified. Much less so for finding something you have not already seen everywhere.

## 7. Galeries Lafayette, breadth of offer

The department store covers every segment, from contemporary to luxury, online and in store. It is the broadest offer on this list, with the usual trade-off, a selection that sets you apart from no one and brands available at every competitor.

## How to choose based on what you want

Three criteria settle it in most cases.

- **Curation.** If the goal is a piece you will not see on three other people at the same party, you need a site that selects, not a site that lists. Concept store rather than marketplace.
- **The physical network.** In ready-to-wear, size and cut remain the leading reason for returns. A site backed by stores lets you try, collect on site and return in person. That is a real edge over a pure player.
- **The returns policy.** Free returns, actual deadline, number of returns allowed per order. That is what determines your risk when ordering a size you have not tried.
