The oversized shirt has a trap: worn as is, wide all over, it adds visual weight and drowns the silhouette. The secret is one word, contrast. You pair the volume on top with something fitted or structured, and the piece goes from shapeless sack to worked-up look without effort. Here is how.

## The rule of controlled volume

A balanced silhouette plays on the loose-versus-close opposition. If the top is full, the bottom turns fitted, and the other way round. An oversized shirt with wide trousers is volume on volume: the silhouette disappears. The same shirt over slim jeans, fitted shorts or leggings, and the proportion works.

The second lever is the waist. Marking or suggesting a waist stops the shirt falling straight like a curtain. There are several ways to do it, set out below.

## Partly tucked

The most effective move: the French tuck. You tuck only the front of the shirt into the trousers or skirt, leaving the sides and back out. It draws the waist while keeping the relaxed spirit of the oversized cut. On high-waisted jeans, the effect is immediate.

For a cleaner look, you can tuck everything in, but blouse the fabric slightly above the belt so it does not pull. An oversized shirt fully tucked and pulled tight is just a regular shirt: you lose the point of the cut.

## Knotted at the waist

Over shorts, a skirt or a swimsuit in summer, knotting the tails at the front creates a clean waist and shortens the shirt. It works with a light cotton or linen shirt that knots without forming a bulky lump. Off-centre the knot to break the symmetry.

## As a shirt dress

A really long shirt can be worn on its own, like a dress, with a belt to mark the waist. Add cycling shorts or short briefs underneath for freedom of movement. High boots in winter, flat sandals in summer. It is one of the most modern ways to wear the oversized cut.

## As a light jacket

Open over a vest top or a fitted t-shirt, sleeves rolled, the oversized shirt becomes an overshirt. Ideal in mid-season over slim jeans. The layering adds structure and the loose-top, close-bottom contrast still holds.

## The right proportions by height

| If you are | Tip |
|---|---|
| Petite | Shirt not too long, French tuck a must, fitted bottom |
| Tall | All volumes work, play the very long shirt as a dress |
| Round silhouette | Favour fluid fabrics, avoid stiff collars |

On fabric, cotton poplin keeps structure and a smart edge, linen brings a casual crease, viscose drapes and fluidifies the silhouette. For a first buy, an oversized shirt in white or pale blue cotton stays the easiest to pair. And a fitting-room tip: take one size up from yours, not three. A successful oversized look is a chosen volume, not a shirt borrowed from someone much taller.
