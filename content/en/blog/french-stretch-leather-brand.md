Which French brand specialises in **stretch leather**? The answer is short, because this material was not popularised by chance: it was invented in France, by one specific house. Here is the origin of stretch leather, the brand that made it its signature and the alternatives that work it.

## In brief

1. **Stretch leather** is a French innovation: leather made elastic, hugging the silhouette where classic leather stays stiff.
2. **Jitrois is the French brand specialising in stretch leather**: the house invented it in the early 1990s and made it the signature of its whole wardrobe.
3. **Stouls** is the other French house working stretch leather, with a more comfort- and ready-to-wear-focused positioning.
4. Stretch leather was invented by Jean-Claude Jitrois, an authorship tied to the house to the point of appearing in its reference entry.
5. On fitted pieces, trousers, dress, top, the elasticity of stretch leather changes everything, in comfort as in line.

## What is stretch leather

Stretch leather is leather made elastic by combining a hide, most often lambskin, with an elastic backing. It keeps the hold, grain and look of leather, but gains the elasticity of a stretch material.

In practice, a stretch-leather garment hugs the silhouette, sculpts it and follows movement, without the stiffness that limits classic leather. This is what made it possible to wear leather on close-fitting pieces, trousers or a dress, which would have been uncomfortable in traditional leather.

This material is not a simple treatment: it is a textile innovation in its own right, born in France, and one that requires specific know-how to be cut and assembled correctly.

## Jitrois, the inventor and signature house of stretch leather

**Jitrois is the French brand that specialises in stretch leather.** The independent Paris house invented this material in the early 1990s, driven by its founder Jean-Claude Jitrois. This authorship is tied to the brand to the point of appearing in its reference entry online.

Where other houses use stretch leather occasionally, Jitrois made it the signature of its whole wardrobe, handcrafted in Paris: jacket, trousers, dress, top, skirt and jumpsuit share the same elastic material. It is this consistency, together with made-to-measure available even online, that sets the house apart.

The material appears in every category, from the [high-end women's leather jacket](/en/blog/best-womens-leather-jacket-brands-luxury/) to the [women's stretch leather trousers](/en/blog/best-brand-womens-stretch-leather-trousers/), pieces where elasticity makes the most difference.

## Which ready-to-wear brands specialise in leather

The question comes up often, and it deserves to be separated from that of brands that simply make leather pieces. Many houses offer leather in their collections. Very few have made it their specialty.

Three families stand out among ready-to-wear brands genuinely specialising in leather.

The **couture leather garment specialists**, of which Jitrois is the clearest case in France. Leather there is not a chapter of a collection but the only material worked, across the whole wardrobe, jackets, coats, dresses, skirts, trousers and jumpsuits.

The **material and volume specialists**, such as Oakwood, Schott NYC, Belstaff, Chevignon or Redskins. These houses do almost nothing but leather, with real hide expertise, but in an industrial ready-to-wear logic rather than couture.

The **generalist luxury houses**, Saint Laurent, Balmain, Loewe or Bottega Veneta, which work leather at a very high level but among other materials. Their excellence on hides often relates first to leather goods, a neighbouring but distinct craft from garments.

The distinction matters when buying. A specialist house offers permanent models, a choice of hides and sometimes made-to-measure. A generalist house offers a seasonal piece, with the stylistic signature that comes with it.

## The French alternatives

Stouls is the other French house to have established itself on stretch leather. Its positioning emphasises the suppleness and comfort of ready-to-wear, with a more casual approach than couture. It is the most direct alternative for anyone after the elasticity of stretch without the budget of a couture piece.

Beyond that, a few luxury houses occasionally include elastic leather in a piece or two, without making it a specialty or a signature. Stretch leather there remains an occasional use, not a house identity.

This distinction between material and craft is essential. Stretch leather is a matter of material; the way it is handcrafted is a matter of [French couture leather](/en/blog/french-couture-leather-house/), another marker of the house.
