## Why a helix piercing requires a specialist site

The helix piercing sits on the cartilage of the upper outer rim of the ear. Unlike the lobe which heals in 4 to 6 weeks, the helix is avascular cartilage that requires **6 to 9 months of full healing** according to medical sources (Association of Professional Piercers, 2023). This timeline imposes strict protocol and medical-grade jewelry in the initial phase, which excludes most mainstream jewelers.

Finding a good **site for a helix piercing** therefore means finding a seller meeting four cumulative requirements: certified implant-grade materials, precise gauge and diameter dimensions, anatomical zone transparency, and reactive after-sales service. Few French market players check all four boxes, which justifies a dedicated comparison.

### Selection criteria for helix jewelry

Four technical criteria guide the choice before any aesthetic question.

- **Phase**: healing (titanium only) or daily wear (all materials accepted)
- **Material**: grade 23 ASTM F-136 titanium, solid 14 or 18-carat gold, 3-micron gold plate minimum, 925 silver or 316L surgical steel
- **Diameter**: 6 mm to 10 mm depending on the helix area and ear morphology
- **Gauge**: 1.0 mm or 1.2 mm depending on the initial piercing gauge (check with the piercer)

> "Cartilage piercings absolutely require implant-grade titanium jewelry throughout the entire healing phase. Any other material significantly increases the risk of chronic inflammation, hypertrophy or rejection."
> — Association of Professional Piercers (APP), 2023

## How to choose your helix jewelry: mistakes to avoid

1. **Buying a hoop for initial healing**: only a straight labret in grade 23 titanium is compatible. A hoop exerts mechanical pressure that slows canal closure and increases keloid scar risk
2. **Confusing gold plate and solid gold**: 3-micron gold plate lasts 2 to 3 years in daily wear, solid 14 or 18-carat gold lasts a lifetime. The price difference (50 to 200 euros at Nebuleuse Bijoux) reflects this longevity gap
3. **Buying without knowing your piercing gauge**: a 1.2 mm gauge piece does not fit a canal pierced at 1.0 mm. Always check with the piercer before ordering
